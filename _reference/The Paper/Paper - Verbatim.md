AbstractAI Summary
To solve the problem of resource consumption and information security during color image transmission, multiple color images encryption scheme according to a three-dimensional discrete chaotic map with pseudo-random number signal generator and discrete wavelet transform (DWT) compression is proposed. Firstly, dynamics of three-dimensional discrete chaotic map is analyzed and found to provide better randomness for encryption schemes. Next, multiple color images of different sizes are compressed to 1/4 of the original size after DWT processing. The multiple compressed images are merged into a plaintext cube, and the plaintext parameters associated with the cube are generated. The generated plaintext parameters are combined with the chaotic map to form the key. A series of sequences are generated by iteration for image confusion and diffusion to get cipher images. The final simulation results show that recovered plaintext image is still clearly visible even with lossy DWT compression. The security analysis results indicate that this scheme has high level of security protection for color images.

1 Introduction

Digital images are becoming increasingly significant in daily life [17]. And with the popularity of internet communication, a large amount of image data is facing serious security challenges. In this case, the protection of the image is particularly important [1, 40]. Currently, there are many schemes for image protection, for example, digital watermarking [43, 44], access control [42], information hiding [48], image encryption [2, 6]. Among these, image encryption is more widely used [13, 38, 52, 55, 57], where encryption destroys the correlation between images to form a noise-like image [4], directly destroys the readability of image content, blocking unauthorized access at its root and providing the highest level of confidentiality assurance, thus protecting the information of the image. With the increase in the volume of information, a single image may not be able to satisfy the demand for information, and multiple images are required to provide information together to satisfy the demand; hence, multiple images are developed to encrypt together. Lin et al. proposed a high-quality image privacy protection scheme based on discrete cosine transform (DCT) frequency domain compression and nonlinear dynamics [32]. Liao et al. proposed combining chaos-driven fractal sorting matrices with Fibonacci Q-matrix techniques to protect information privacy [56]. Zhang et al. employed a synergistic approach combining a 4D fractional-order Hopfield neural network with a 2D hyperchaotic map to generate a key stream, thereby providing robust protection for medical images [11]. Li et al. combined vector-level operations with the chaotic properties of 2D-ELMM to encrypt images [29]. Wei et al. further developed the 2D-SQPM and pixel fusion strategy into an efficient image encryption algorithm [10]. Lai et al. propose a novel two-dimensional hyperchaotic map for image encryption, derived from classical sine and Gaussian map [26]. Lai et al. combined the integer wavelet transform with DNA encoding to encrypt medical images [25]. Gao et al. introduced a 3D memristive cubic map with dual discrete memristor to image encryption [14]. Gao et al. designed a parallel color image encryption algorithm based on chaotic map [15].

Chaotic map has been widely used in the image encryption by virtue of their high nonlinear and sensitivity to initial conditions [23, 54]. Better randomness makes the image resistant to statistical attacks. Chaotic map includes continuous chaotic map [9, 20], fractional order chaotic map [33, 45], and discrete chaotic map [3, 36]. Discrete chaotic map is fast in iteration and hence is used in image encryption [34]. It is used in this article and encrypts images by iteratively generating a series of chaotic sequences to secure the information of the image.

Long channel transmission time is also a problem that needs to be solved while designing multi-image encryption schemes. The size of the image data can be reduced with the help of compression techniques, thus reducing the bandwidth required for transmission. Researchers have developed a range of compression and encryption schemes around this problem. The compressed image was encrypted using a chaotic map [5]; Wen et al. divided the image, converted the time domain to the frequency domain using DCT, and compressed the image extracting the DC and AC coefficients from the DCT coefficient matrix [47]; Tan et al. used the sparse set of DCT combined with chaotic map to compress and encrypt [41]. Liao et al. proposed a framework based on facial muscle movements to address the problem of detecting compressed deepfake videos [31]. Liao et al. proposed a cascaded network in the wavelet domain, which demonstrates strong capability in detecting watermark information within this domain. It exhibits sensitivity to both high-frequency and low-frequency details, thereby providing effective protection for sensitive information [12]. Li et al. have proposed an algorithm for screen-shooting resistant watermarking with grayscale deviation simulation that outperforms existing methods in both robustness and visual quality [30]. Chen et al. propose a signal noise separation-based (SNIS) network for tampered region localization and semantic tampering operation classification, SNIS achieves competitive and robustness detection performance in different image forgery cases [7]. These schemes are designed to compress the size of the images for transmission, but are relatively ineffective in recovering compressed images, and the time required for compression perception is relatively long. In this article, for three channels R, G, B of multiple color images with varying sizes, each channel is divided into four components using DWT and the information of the LL component is encrypted. The original image is compressed to 1/4. The compressed images are merged into a cube and the images are encrypted in the cube to obtain the cipher cube as well as the cipher image.

The major innovations of this article are described below.

(1) Multiple color images of different sizes are compressed using DWT, and the resulting compressed reconstructed images are visually clearer.
(2) Compatibility is achieved by encrypting multiple color images of different sizes in a cube.
(3) The performance and attack resistance of the encryption scheme were tested. The results indicate some advantages over other similar schemes.

The sections of the article are distributed as follows: The dynamics of three-dimensional discrete chaotic map is analyzed in Section 2. The design of the pseudo-random number generator is shown in Section 3. The whole process of encryption scheme is analyzed in Section 4. The simulation is analyzed in Section 5. The results of the safety analysis are presented in Section 6. Section 7 contains the conclusions.

4 Encryption and Decryption Scheme

4.1 Encryption Scheme

Firstly, multiple color images are input, then three channels (R, G, B) of multiple color images are compressed using DWT technique, LL sub-bands are retained. Multiple LL sub-bands are merged into a large cube to form the compressed plaintext cube C. Parameters associated with the plaintext are generated based on the height of C. These parameters are combined with the parameters and initial values of the chaotic map, which together form the key. Finally, the cipher cube D is generated. 6.3 Statistical Characteristic Analysis

6.3.1 Histogram Analysis.

It is a fundamental tool for statistical analysis of images, as it reflects the frequency distribution of pixel values. Histogram of plaintext image is usually unevenly distributed, with high concentration of certain pixel values and low frequency of others. This distributional feature directly exposes the statistical specificities about pixel values. However, cipher image is nearly uniformly distributed, and no identifiable features appear, which effectively prevents the leakage of information from the plaintext image. 7 Conclusion

This article introduces an encryption scheme according to a pseudo-random number signal generator of three-dimensional discrete chaotic map and DWT compression. Firstly, by analyzing the phase, LEs, bifurcation diagrams, and SE of three-dimensional discrete chaotic map, it was found that they have better dynamical behavior and can provide better randomness for encryption schemes. The color images are compressed by DWT to 1/4 of the plaintext image. Then all compressed images are merged into a plaintext cube and encrypted in the cube. Finally, the simulation results show a higher quality of reduction compared to similar compression schemes. The security analysis shows that the scheme has a bigger key space, can resistant to brute force cracking, and it is resistant to differential attacks, statistical attacks, noise and shear attacks.

For extra pixels in the cube, the scheme is padded with zeros, which takes up space, and there is a need to reduce the utilization of the extra space in future encryption schemes to make the new encryption scheme more robust and less resource-intensive.
